# Tax-Saving Levers — AY 2026-27 (FY 2025-26)

Legit tax planning only. No evasion, no fake receipts, no phantom deductions. If the lever below relies on something you actually did or actually paid for, use it. If not, don't.

Every lever is tagged by regime: `[OLD]` = old-regime only; `[NEW]` = new-regime only; `[BOTH]` = works under both.

Cross-check each against `references/regimes.md` for the exact caps and eligibility.

---

## 1. Regime selection — the biggest single lever

- **Compute BOTH regimes for every filing.** The winner isn't a rule of thumb — it depends on your deduction profile. See `regimes.md §4` for a worked example.
- **Break-even heuristic (salaried, no CG):**
  - Total old-regime deductions (80C + 80D + HRA + 24(b) + std ded − new-regime std ded) < ₹3L → New wins.
  - Deductions ₹3–5L → close; compute exactly.
  - Deductions > ₹5L → Old usually wins.
- **Special-rate income breaks the heuristic.** LTCG > ₹1.25L, STCG on listed equity, dividend income — all taxed at flat rates in both regimes; slab-rate difference doesn't matter for that slice. If most income is capital gains, regime choice barely moves the needle.
- **Belated filing forces new regime** — file by 31-Jul-2026 to preserve the choice.

## 2. New regime — allowed levers `[NEW]`

Very few — that's the trade-off. Every rupee you get through these is real value.

### 2a. Sec 80CCD(2) — Employer NPS up to 14% of Basic+DA `[NEW]`
- Cap under new regime = **14% of (Basic + DA)** for BOTH private and Central Govt employees.
- Old regime cap for private-sector employees = 10%.
- **How to pull this lever:** Ask your employer's payroll to route part of your CTC to NPS Tier-I (employer contribution). Salary structure change — usually annual restructuring window. NPS Tier-I only (Tier-II doesn't qualify).
- **Real value:** On ₹30L Basic+DA, 14% = ₹4.2L deduction. At 30% marginal rate + cess = **₹1.31L/year tax saved**.
- **Trap:** Locks the money until age 60 (Tier-I withdrawal restrictions). Only 60% lump-sum tax-free at withdrawal; 40% must go into annuity. Do the NPV before locking.

### 2b. Sec 80CCH(2) — Agniveer Corpus Fund `[NEW]`
- Only for Agniveers or their dependants. Not applicable to civilian salaried.

### 2c. Family pension standard deduction `[NEW]`
- If receiving family pension, deduction u/s 57(iia) = lower of ₹25,000 or 1/3rd of pension. Raised from ₹15,000 by FA(No.2) 2024.

### 2d. Home loan interest on LET-OUT property `[NEW]`
- Sec 24(b) interest deductible against rental income even under new regime.
- **BUT:** resulting HP loss cannot offset salary under new regime (Sec 115BAC(2)(i)); forced to Schedule CFL for c/f under Sec 71B (8 years, HP income only).
- Net effect: interest deduction only matters if rental income covers it. If interest > rent by ₹1L, that ₹1L is locked away for years hoping for future rental income to absorb.

### 2e. Employer contributions that stay out of taxable salary `[NEW]`
Not "deductions" strictly, but structural choices that reduce Schedule S gross salary:
- **EPF employer contribution** — exempt up to 12% of Basic. Above 12% becomes perquisite.
- **Gratuity provision** — not taxed till payout (subject to Sec 10(10) cap on eventual receipt).
- **Superannuation employer contribution** — exempt up to ₹1.5L p.a. under FA 2020 (aggregate 80CCD(2) + Sec 17(2)(vii) cap of ₹7.5L applies).
- **Meal vouchers / food coupons** — Sec 17(2) exemption ₹50/meal capped at 22 working days = ~₹26,400/yr. Old regime only actually — new regime disallows under Sec 115BAC. Verify with employer.

## 3. Old regime — allowed levers `[OLD]`

The full deduction stack. If you're under old regime and NOT maxing these, you're paying more tax than needed.

### 3a. Sec 80C — ₹1.5L cap `[OLD]`
Priority order by lock-in / return / liquidity:
1. **EPF employee contribution** — auto-consumed; you can't avoid it. Counts toward 80C.
2. **PPF** — 15-year lock, 7.1% tax-free. Excellent for conservative allocation.
3. **ELSS** — 3-year lock, equity, potential returns highest. LTCG on redemption taxable u/s 112A.
4. **NPS Tier-I (employee contribution)** — counts here up to 10% of salary; but the extra ₹50K under 80CCD(1B) is a better slot (below).
5. **5-year tax-saver FD** — 5-year lock, taxable interest.
6. **Life insurance** — only if you actually need the cover. Do not buy for tax saving alone.
7. **SSY (Sukanya Samriddhi)** — only if you have a girl child < 10.
8. **Home loan principal + tuition fees + NSC** — usually consumed alongside the above.

**Fill 80C to ₹1.5L exactly** — additional contribution above ₹1.5L into these instruments is wasted deduction-wise.

### 3b. Sec 80CCD(1B) — additional NPS ₹50K `[OLD]`
- Over and above the ₹1.5L 80CCE cap.
- Tier-I self contribution, any age.
- **Real value:** ₹50K deduction × 30% = ₹15K + cess. Do this every year if in old regime.

### 3c. Sec 80CCD(2) — Employer NPS 10% `[OLD]`
- Cap for private-sector under old regime = **10% of (Basic+DA)**. Central Govt = 14%.
- Same lever as `§2a` but with a lower cap.

### 3d. Sec 80D — Health insurance `[OLD]`
- Self + spouse + dependent children: ₹25K (₹50K if any is senior).
- Parents: separate ₹25K (₹50K if senior).
- Preventive health check-up: ₹5K sub-cap inside the overall limit.
- **Max stack:** ₹1L if both you and parents are senior citizens.
- Buy for actual coverage, not tax. If you already have employer coverage, top-up for parents.

### 3e. HRA — Sec 10(13A) `[OLD]`
- Exempt = least of: (a) actual HRA; (b) 50% of Basic+DA if metro / 40% if non-metro; (c) rent paid − 10% of Basic+DA.
- **Metros for this rule** = Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai only. Bangalore, Hyderabad, Pune are NOT metros for HRA. Common mistake.
- **Landlord PAN mandatory if annual rent > ₹1L.** Missing PAN → landlord declaration OR HRA denied.
- **Rent to parents is allowed** — set up a formal rent agreement, transfer rent monthly via bank, parents disclose rental income on their return, deduct 30% std deduction on parents' side. Family arbitrage: your marginal 30% saved, parents at 5-20% band.
- **HRA cannot be claimed with Sec 80GG.**

### 3f. Sec 24(b) — Home loan interest, self-occupied ₹2L `[OLD]`
- Interest on home loan for self-occupied property, capped at ₹2L/yr.
- Under-construction: interest accumulated pre-completion is deductible in 5 equal installments starting the year of completion (Sec 24(b) proviso), within the ₹2L cap.
- If let-out: full interest deductible, but HP loss set-off capped at ₹2L against other heads (Sec 71(3A)), balance c/f 8 years.

### 3g. Sec 80E — Education loan interest `[OLD]`
- **No cap.** Full interest deductible for 8 assessment years from year of first interest payment OR until loan is fully repaid, whichever earlier.
- Loan must be for higher education (self, spouse, children, legal ward) from approved institution / financial institution.
- Interest only — principal not deductible.

### 3h. Sec 80EE / 80EEA — Extra home loan interest `[OLD]`
- 80EE: extra ₹50K if loan sanctioned Apr-2016 to Mar-2017, loan ≤ ₹35L, property ≤ ₹50L, first-time buyer.
- 80EEA: extra ₹1.5L if sanctioned Apr-2019 to Mar-2022, stamp value ≤ ₹45L, first-time buyer.
- Both are one-time eligibility windows now closed for new loans — check original loan sanction date.

### 3i. Sec 80G — Donations `[OLD]`
- **Cash > ₹2,000 disallowed** (Sec 80G(5D)) — always pay non-cash.
- **Form 10BE with ARN mandatory** from donee. No ARN → no deduction, portal blocks.
- 100% deduction w/o qualifying limit: PM CARES, PMNRF, NDF, CM Relief Fund.
- Other institutions: 50% or 100% deduction, subject to 10% of adjusted GTI cap.

### 3j. Sec 80TTA / 80TTB `[OLD]`
- 80TTA: ₹10K savings-account interest deduction (non-senior).
- 80TTB: ₹50K aggregate deposits interest (savings + FD/RD combined) for senior citizens. Excludes 80TTA.

### 3k. Sec 80GG — Rent without HRA `[OLD]`
- Only if no HRA received.
- Least of: ₹5K/month, 25% of adjusted TI, rent − 10% of adjusted TI.
- Form 10BA declaration required.
- Usually inferior to HRA if HRA is available — don't quit HRA voluntarily.

## 4. Cross-regime levers `[BOTH]`

### 4a. Sec 54EC — LTCG bonds on immovable property `[BOTH]`
- Invest LTCG from sale of immovable property (any asset actually — Sec 54EC covers land or building or both) in NHAI, REC, PFC, or IRFC bonds within **6 months of transfer**.
- Cap: **₹50L per FY** (aggregate across all bonds).
- **5-year lock-in** (was 3 years pre-2018).
- Bond interest ~5.25%, taxable.
- **When it makes sense:** you have LTCG > ₹50L on property, don't want the reinvestment restriction of 54F, and can lock ₹50L for 5 years at low return in exchange for saving 12.5% on that ₹50L (= ₹6.25L tax saved). NPV positive if your alternative return > tax saved over the lock.

### 4b. Sec 54F — LTCG reinvestment in residential property `[BOTH]`
- On sale of any long-term capital asset OTHER than a residential house.
- Reinvest **net consideration** (not just gain) in a residential house — either purchased within 2 years (or 1 year prior) OR constructed within 3 years.
- Full exemption if entire net consideration is reinvested; proportionate exemption otherwise.
- **Cap since FA 2023:** cost of new house exceeding ₹10 crore is ignored for computation → cap at ₹10Cr new-house cost.
- Restriction: cannot own more than 1 residential house (other than the new one) on transfer date.
- **When it makes sense:** you were going to buy a house anyway and have a large non-property LTCG (e.g., business sale, IPO exit). Free tax shelter.

### 4c. Sec 54 — LTCG reinvestment when the SOLD asset is a residential house `[BOTH]`
- Sale of long-term residential house → buy/construct another within same timelines as 54F.
- Reinvest LTCG (not full consideration) → exemption to that extent.
- One-time option: reinvest in TWO houses if LTCG ≤ ₹2Cr (FA 2019 amendment).
- **Cap since FA 2023:** ₹10Cr cost of new house.

### 4d. Capital loss harvesting before 31-Mar `[BOTH]`
- Book losses on losing positions before 31-Mar to offset gains from winning positions.
- Wash-sale rule: **India has no wash-sale rule** — you can sell at a loss and rebuy the same security next day (or same day on a different lot). Losses are validly claimed even after rebuy.
- **Priority:**
  1. STCL first — offsets STCG (20%) or LTCG (12.5%). Best rate save.
  2. LTCL only offsets LTCG (12.5%). Save if you have LTCG > ₹1.25L to shelter.
- **Trap:** VDA (crypto) losses die alone. Cannot harvest against anything, cannot c/f. Don't bother.

### 4e. Timing capital gains — cross fiscal years `[BOTH]`
- If your LTCG under 112A is ~₹1.25L this year, book gains up to ₹1.25L before 31-Mar (free of tax due to exemption), rebuy on 1-Apr → higher cost base for future.
- If you have ₹2L LTCG and no LTCL, holding to next FY doesn't help — the threshold is per year, so gain > ₹1.25L is taxable this year AND next year's ₹1.25L exemption is preserved.
- If you have ₹5L STCG and can defer sale by 1 day past the 12-month mark → STCG @ 20% becomes LTCG under 112A @ 12.5% (post threshold). Verify holding period exactly.

### 4f. Real-estate LTCG election (pre-23-Jul-2024 acquisitions) `[BOTH]`
- Resident individual / HUF only.
- Property acquired before 23-Jul-2024 sold on/after 23-Jul-2024 → compute both:
  - 12.5% × LTCG (no indexation)
  - 20% × LTCG (with indexation using CII table for FY 2025-26)
- Pick the lower. Election is row-level in Schedule CG B10.
- **The indexation option often wins for property held > 5 years** — high inflation × long holding = high indexed cost base = low taxable gain.

### 4g. Foreign tax credit — file Form 67 on time `[BOTH]`
- Not a deduction, but preserves credit that would otherwise be disallowed.
- File Form 67 online BEFORE ITR upload (or same day). Rule 128(9). See `foreign-assets.md §4`.
- On ₹10L US dividend income with 25% US withholding = ₹2.5L FTC. Miss Form 67 = CPC disallows the ₹2.5L, you pay full Indian slab tax on it, then litigate to recover. Filing Form 67 is a 20-minute exercise.

### 4h. Advance tax to avoid 234B / 234C interest `[BOTH]`
- If tax liability net of TDS > ₹10K, pay advance tax in installments (15% by 15-Jun, 45% cumulative by 15-Sep, 75% by 15-Dec, 100% by 15-Mar).
- Miss → interest @ 1%/month.
- **Salaried with big Q4 capital gain or foreign dividend:** TDS won't cover; pay ad-hoc advance tax by 15-Mar for the CG portion (Sec 234C proviso — advance tax on CG is due in the installment AFTER the transaction, not backdated).

### 4i. Pre-file capital loss preservation `[BOTH]`
- File by 31-Jul-2026 to preserve capital loss c/f under Sec 80.
- If your inputs aren't complete but you have material CL: advance-pay estimated tax, file by 31-Jul with best-effort numbers, then revise under Sec 139(5) before 31-Dec-2026 with clean numbers.
- Belated = c/f lost. See `losses.md §4`.

## 5. Aggressive-but-legit tactics

### 5a. Rent to parents `[OLD, if you take HRA]`
- Formal rent agreement, monthly bank transfer, parents declare rental income (30% std deduction on their side, then their slab).
- If parents are seniors with low income, arbitrage of 30% (your slab) vs 5% (their slab) on the rent amount.
- Not evasion — the rent is real and taxed on the recipient. Structure must be genuine.

### 5b. Split investments in spouse's name (limited utility)
- Clubbing (Sec 60-64) means income from assets you transferred to spouse is added back to yours.
- Exception: **investment made from spouse's own funds** (her salary, her inheritance) is her income entirely. Don't gift to spouse for tax-splitting — clubbing kicks in.

### 5c. Family HUF (Hindu Undivided Family)
- Separate PAN, separate ₹2.5L basic exemption, own 80C limit.
- Useful only if you have ancestral income (inheritance, gift from ancestors) or joint family assets.
- Cannot artificially fund with your salary income.

### 5d. NPS Tier-I extra ₹50K `[OLD]`
- Even if you're already maxing 80C via other instruments, add ₹50K to NPS Tier-I annually for the 80CCD(1B) slot. Same lock-in tradeoff as 80CCD(2) but with self contribution.

### 5e. Corporate NPS opt-in `[NEW especially]`
- Ask HR if the company offers Corporate NPS. If yes, restructure to route 14% of Basic+DA there.
- Some employers auto-limit at 10% for old regime; get them to route 14% and file under new regime to unlock the delta.

### 5f. Sell losing US RSU lots before 31-Mar `[BOTH]`
- If any specific vest lot is under water (rare in 2020-2024 bull run, but possible for 2022 vests), sell → STCL (< 24 mo) offsets other STCG at slab; LTCL (> 24 mo) offsets LTCG @ 12.5%.
- No wash-sale rule in India — rebuy same stock 1 day later.

## 5B. Business-income levers `[ITR-3, BOTH regimes]`

If the taxpayer has F&O, freelance, consulting, or other business/professional income, these levers apply in addition to §1-4 above.

### 5B-a. All actual business expenses `[BOTH]`
Every rupee spent for business is deductible under Schedule PL. For F&O / consultants specifically:
- **Brokerage + STT + exchange fees + GST + SEBI + stamp duty** on F&O trades — fully deductible in Schedule PL.
- **Internet, phone, software subscriptions** (trading platform, charting tool, cloud subscriptions) — deductible in proportion to business use.
- **Home office** — proportion of rent, electricity, internet based on area used exclusively for business. Defensible only if provably exclusive (dedicated room, not shared).
- **Laptop / trading rig depreciation** — 40% p.a. on computers if capitalised as business asset (Schedule DPM).
- **Professional fees paid** (CA, consultant) — deductible with TDS obligation u/s 194J if > ₹30,000/annum to single professional.
- **Travel for business** — client meetings, conferences.

**Trap:** Personal expenses debited to business account. Sec 37(1) explicitly disallows. Rule of thumb: if you'd have spent it anyway (personal Netflix, personal groceries), don't route through business.

### 5B-b. 44AD election — trade-off `[ITR-3]`
- If your F&O profit is consistently > 6% of turnover and turnover ≤ ₹3 Cr, 44AD election eliminates books requirement AND audit requirement.
- But **5-year lock-out** on opting out (Sec 44AD(4)): once you elect 44AD and later go back to actual profits (declaring less than 6%), Sec 44AB(e) audit is triggered for the next 5 years if income > basic exemption.
- **When it makes sense:** consistently profitable smaller trader, turnover < ₹1 Cr, no plans to scale or diversify.
- **When it doesn't:** volatile profitability (typical F&O), any expectation of future loss year, high turnover approaching ₹3 Cr cap.
- See `references/fno-speculative.md §6` for detailed analysis.

### 5B-c. Sec 43B timing lever `[ITR-3]`
- Statutory dues (GST, PF, ESI, gratuity, interest to banks) are deductible only in the year of actual payment (Sec 43B).
- **Lever:** pay these before 31-March to claim deduction in current FY; delayed payment shifts deduction to next FY.
- Practical only for freelancers with staff / GST liability. F&O-only filer usually has no 43B items.

### 5B-d. Additional depreciation on new plant `[ITR-3, manufacturing]`
- Sec 32(1)(iia): extra 20% depreciation in year of purchase for new eligible plant & machinery in a manufacturing/production business.
- Half-rate (10%) if used < 180 days in first year.
- Not applicable to office equipment, furniture, vehicles, or trading-only businesses.

### 5B-e. Advance tax quarters for business income `[ITR-3]`
- Non-presumptive business: standard quarterly (15-Jun 15%, 15-Sep 45%, 15-Dec 75%, 15-Mar 100%).
- **Presumptive 44AD / 44ADA:** entire liability payable by **15-March** — single installment (Sec 211 proviso). Skip earlier installments without 234C interest.
- F&O windfall in Q4: Sec 234C proviso — advance tax on unexpected Q4 profit is due 15-March, not backdated.

### 5B-f. Form 10-IEA regime election — lifetime consequence `[ITR-3]`
- Not a "saving lever" in isolation — but a critical decision with permanent effect.
- ITR-3 filer opting for Old regime files 10-IEA before 31-Jul-2026 (or 31-Oct if audit).
- Can switch back to New **only ONCE lifetime**. After switch-back, Old is barred forever.
- **Before electing Old:** compute both regimes for AT LEAST 3 forward-looking scenarios (current + likely income trajectory). Only elect Old if it wins by ≥ ₹50K AND you don't expect income to grow into 30% slab dominance.

## 6. What NOT to do

Avoid these. All are either evasion, deliberate misreporting, or legally risky:

- **Fake rent receipts to a fictitious landlord** — CBDT circular allows verification; portal cross-checks landlord PAN against filed returns. Scrutiny catches this.
- **Bogus 80G donations to a shell trust** — Form 10BE ARN verification killed this route in 2022.
- **Under-reporting foreign dividend** — AIS captures CRS/FATCA-shared data. NUDGE campaigns 2024/2025 caught ₹29,208 Cr of undisclosed foreign assets.
- **Skipping Schedule FA because "the account has only $100"** — no de-minimis for disclosure; ₹10L penalty under Sec 42 BMA regardless (the ₹20L de-minimis is for penalty relief on non-immovable-property assets, not disclosure).
- **Claiming HRA + Sec 80GG together** — mutually exclusive; auto-flagged.
- **Claiming Sec 80C > ₹1.5L** — hard cap; excess is ignored, not carried over.
- **Filing belated to "wait for AIS to update"** — Sec 80 kills loss c/f; ₹5,000 penalty under Sec 234F; forced new regime. Just file with best numbers and revise.
