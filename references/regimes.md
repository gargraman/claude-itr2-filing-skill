# Old Regime vs New Regime — FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27)

**Default:** New regime u/s 115BAC(1A) from AY 2024-25. To use old regime, the taxpayer must actively opt out.

**Opt-out mechanics for AY 2026-27:**
- **Salaried, no business/profession income (typical ITR-2 filer):** No Form 10-IEA required. Simply toggle the "opting out of new regime u/s 115BAC" checkbox to Yes in Part A-GEN of ITR-2. Toggleable year-to-year, unlimited times.
- **With business/profession income (ITR-3 filer):** Form 10-IEA MANDATORY, filed online **before the ITR due date u/s 139(1)** — 31-July-2026 for non-audit, 31-October-2026 for audit case. If filed after this date, opt-out is invalid and return is treated under New regime by default.
  - **Lifetime switch-back restriction (Sec 115BAC(6)):** Once an ITR-3 filer has opted out of New regime via 10-IEA, they can switch back to New regime **only ONCE lifetime**. After that switch-back, Old regime is **permanently barred** for that PAN — cannot re-elect for any future AY. This is the single most consequential decision an ITR-3 filer makes.
  - **Practical guidance for F&O / consulting filer:** Compute both regimes before filing. Do NOT default to Old just because it was better in a salaried past. If New wins for this AY, stay in New (no 10-IEA). If Old wins by a small margin, weigh the loss of future flexibility vs the one-year saving — often better to stay in New unless Old wins by ≥ ₹50K.
  - **When New later becomes clearly better** (e.g., income grows and 30% slab dominates deductions), the one-time switch-back is available — but that's the last chance. Plan accordingly.
- **Belated return (139(4)):** Old regime CANNOT be elected. Return is locked into new regime. Applies to both salaried (ITR-2) and business (ITR-3) filers.

## 1. New Regime — Sec 115BAC(1A) — FY 2025-26

**Slabs (Finance Act 2025):**

| Total income (₹) | Rate |
|---|---|
| 0 – 4,00,000 | Nil |
| 4,00,001 – 8,00,000 | 5% |
| 8,00,001 – 12,00,000 | 10% |
| 12,00,001 – 16,00,000 | 15% |
| 16,00,001 – 20,00,000 | 20% |
| 20,00,001 – 24,00,000 | 25% |
| Above 24,00,000 | 30% |

**Standard deduction (Sec 16(ia)):** ₹75,000 for salaried and pensioners.

**Sec 87A rebate (FY 2025-26 under new regime):**
- Rebate up to **₹60,000** if total income ≤ ₹12,00,000.
- Marginal relief preserved — at ₹12,10,000 total income, tax ≈ ₹10,000 (not the ~₹61,500 cliff).
- **NOT available** on income taxed at special rates: STCG u/s 111A @ 20%, LTCG u/s 112 @ 12.5%, LTCG u/s 112A @ 12.5%, winnings u/s 115BB, VDA u/s 115BBH, online games u/s 115BBJ.

**Net effect for salaried under new regime:** Zero tax up to **₹12.75 lakh gross salary** (₹12,00,000 rebated + ₹75,000 std deduction).

**Chapter VI-A deductions ALLOWED under new regime:**
- **Sec 80CCD(2)** — Employer NPS contribution. Cap = **14% of Basic+DA** for BOTH private-sector and Central Government employees (raised from 10% for private sector by Finance (No.2) Act 2024, effective AY 2025-26, applies to FY 2025-26). Critical lever for reducing new-regime tax.
- **Sec 80CCH(2)** — Agniveer Corpus Fund contribution.
- **Sec 80JJAA** — Employer deduction for new employee cost (employer-side, not usually salaried individual).
- **Family pension standard deduction u/s 57(iia)** — lower of ₹25,000 or 1/3rd (raised from ₹15,000 by Finance (No.2) Act 2024, applies FY 2025-26).
- Transport allowance for differently-abled, conveyance allowance for official duty, gratuity/leave encashment/PF exemptions per the Act, official-duty perquisites (per Sec 10(14)).
- Home loan interest u/s 24(b) on **let-out** property — allowed as a deduction against rental income. BUT resulting HP loss CANNOT be set off against salary (Sec 115BAC(2)(i)); it's forced to Schedule CFL for c/f 8 years under Sec 71B.

**Chapter VI-A deductions NOT allowed under new regime (portal validation blocks these):**
- Sec 80C, 80CCC, 80CCD(1), 80CCD(1B) — all NPS self + tax-saver instruments
- Sec 80D — health insurance
- Sec 80DD, 80DDB, 80U — disability
- Sec 80E, 80EE, 80EEA — education loan interest, home loan interest (extra)
- Sec 80G — donations (except via employer under specific circulars — rare)
- Sec 80GG — rent for non-HRA earners
- Sec 80TTA — savings acct interest ₹10K
- Sec 80TTB — senior citizen deposit interest ₹50K
- HRA exemption u/s 10(13A)
- LTA exemption u/s 10(5)
- Interest u/s 24(b) on self-occupied house (₹2L limit) — blocked
- Home-loan principal within 80C — blocked (as part of 80C block)

## 2. Old Regime — FY 2025-26

**Slabs (unchanged for years):**

| Total income (₹) | Rate |
|---|---|
| 0 – 2,50,000 | Nil |
| 2,50,001 – 5,00,000 | 5% |
| 5,00,001 – 10,00,000 | 20% |
| Above 10,00,000 | 30% |

**Standard deduction (Sec 16(ia)):** ₹50,000 for salaried and pensioners.

**Sec 87A rebate:** ₹12,500 max if total income ≤ ₹5,00,000. Marginal relief preserved. NOT on special-rate income.

**Chapter VI-A deductions (main ones for salaried individual, <60):**

| Section | Cap | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **80C + 80CCC + 80CCD(1)** aggregate under Sec 80CCE | ₹1,50,000 | Instruments: PPF, EPF (employee contribution), ELSS mutual funds, LIC premium, NSC, 5-year tax-saver FD, SSY, home-loan principal, children's tuition (max 2 kids), NPS Tier-I employee contribution (10% of salary employee-side within this cap). |
| **80CCD(1B)** | ₹50,000 (over & above 80C limit) | Additional NPS Tier-I self-contribution. Uniquely allowed above 80C cap. |
| **80CCD(2)** | 10% of Basic+DA (private employee) / 14% (Central Govt employee) | Employer NPS. Under OLD regime, private-sector cap remains 10%; the 14% enhancement applies ONLY under new regime. |
| **80D** | ₹25,000 self+spouse+dep. children + ₹25,000 parents; each limb becomes ₹50,000 if the covered person is a senior citizen. Preventive health check-up sub-cap ₹5,000 within the overall limit. | Max ₹1,00,000 total (both taxpayer and parents senior). |
| **80DD** | ₹75,000 / ₹1,25,000 (severe disability) | Flat deduction; dependant with disability. |
| **80DDB** | ₹40,000 / ₹1,00,000 (senior) | Specified diseases (cancer, chronic renal failure, etc.); net of insurance/employer reimbursement. |
| **80U** | ₹75,000 / ₹1,25,000 (severe) | Self disability. |
| **80E** | No cap | Interest on education loan; deduction for 8 assessment years from year of first interest payment. |
| **80EE** | Extra ₹50,000 | Home loan interest; loan sanctioned 01-Apr-2016 to 31-Mar-2017; loan ≤ ₹35L; property ≤ ₹50L; first-time home buyer. |
| **80EEA** | Extra ₹1,50,000 | Loan sanctioned 01-Apr-2019 to 31-Mar-2022; stamp-duty value ≤ ₹45L; first-time buyer; not with 80EE. Not extended after Mar-2022. |
| **80G** | 100% or 50%, with/without qualifying limit | PM CARES, PMNRF, NDF, CM Relief Fund = 100% no cap. Cash > ₹2,000 disallowed. ARN from Form 10BE mandatory. |
| **80GG** | Least of ₹5,000/month, 25% of adjusted TI, rent − 10% of adjusted TI | Only if no HRA claimed. Form 10BA required. |
| **80TTA** | ₹10,000 | Savings account interest (banks, post office, cooperative bank). Non-senior. |
| **80TTB** | ₹50,000 | Senior citizens' deposit interest (savings + FD/RD combined). Excludes 80TTA. |
| **Sec 24(b)** | ₹2,00,000 (self-occupied) | Home loan interest on self-occupied house. Loss from HP can be set off against salary up to ₹2L/yr; balance c/f 8 years. |

**HRA exemption (Sec 10(13A))** — old regime only:
Exempt = **least** of:
1. Actual HRA received
2. 50% of salary (Basic + DA) if metro (Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai) — else 40%
3. Rent paid minus 10% of salary (Basic + DA)

Documents: rent receipts, rent agreement, **landlord PAN if annual rent > ₹1,00,000** (CBDT Circular 8/2013). Without PAN — signed landlord declaration.

**LTA exemption (Sec 10(5))** — old regime only. Available twice in a block of 4 calendar years (current block 2022-25 rolls into 2026-29). Only travel fare within India; not stay or food.

## 3. Surcharge (both regimes) — FY 2025-26

Individual slabs:

| Total income (₹) | Surcharge |
|---|---|
| > 50 L to 1 Cr | 10% |
| > 1 Cr to 2 Cr | 15% |
| > 2 Cr to 5 Cr | 25% |
| > 5 Cr | **37% (OLD only); 25% (NEW — capped)** |

**Critical caps (both regimes):**
- **Surcharge on dividend income and on income u/s 111A, 112, 112A, 115AD(1)(b): capped at 15%** regardless of total income or regime.
- Marginal relief at each surcharge threshold.

**Health & Education Cess:** **4% on (income tax + surcharge)**, all regimes, all income levels.

## 4. Regime comparison worked example (₹40L gross salary + ₹1.5L Sec 80C + ₹25K 80D + ₹80K employer NPS 10% + no HRA)

### Old regime:
- Gross salary: ₹40,00,000
- Less: Standard deduction: ₹50,000
- Less: 80C: ₹1,50,000 (max)
- Less: 80D: ₹25,000
- Less: 80CCD(2) employer NPS: ₹80,000 (below 10% cap)
- Total Income: ₹36,95,000
- Tax (slab): 12,500 + 1,00,000 + 8,08,500 = ₹9,21,000
- Rebate 87A: nil (TI > 5L)
- Surcharge: nil (TI < 50L)
- Cess 4%: ₹36,840
- **Total tax: ₹9,57,840**

### New regime:
- Gross salary: ₹40,00,000
- Less: Standard deduction: ₹75,000
- Less: 80CCD(2) employer NPS: ₹1,12,000 (14% cap — assumes employer willing to contribute; if only 10% = ₹80,000, use that)
- Total Income: ₹38,13,000 (with 14% NPS)
- Tax:
  - 0-4L: nil
  - 4-8L: ₹20,000
  - 8-12L: ₹40,000
  - 12-16L: ₹60,000
  - 16-20L: ₹80,000
  - 20-24L: ₹1,00,000
  - 24L-38.13L: ₹4,23,900 @ 30% = ₹4,23,900
  - Total slab tax: ₹7,23,900
- Rebate 87A: nil (TI > 12L)
- Surcharge: nil (TI < 50L)
- Cess 4%: ₹28,956
- **Total tax: ₹7,52,856**

**Savings under new regime: ₹2,04,984**

*In this profile, new regime wins by ~₹2L because the loss of 80C/80D/HRA (~₹1.75L of deductions × 30%) is smaller than the slab-rate benefit (all income above 24L is 30% under both, but bands below 30% under new are wider). Old regime wins when total deductions push OLD taxable income to well below the point where NEW rate structure catches up — roughly when total old-regime deductions > ₹5L for a salaried filer.*

**General rule of thumb (rough — always compute both):**
- Deductions < ₹3L → New regime almost always wins
- Deductions ₹3-5L → Roughly equal; compute
- Deductions > ₹5L → Old regime may win, especially at ₹15-25L income band with heavy 80C + HRA + home loan interest

## 5. AMT — Alternate Minimum Tax (Sec 115JC)

Applies to Old regime only (Sec 115JEE(3) exempts 115BAC filers). AMT @ 18.5% + surcharge + cess on Adjusted Total Income if regular tax < AMT AND ATI > ₹20L. Salaried individual without Sec 10AA / 80H-80RRB deductions almost never triggers AMT. Skip Schedule AMT unless user has SEZ income or specific deductions.

---

## 6. Advance tax (both regimes)

Applicable if total tax liability (net of TDS) > ₹10,000 for the FY.

Installments:
- 15% by 15-Jun-2025
- 45% cumulative by 15-Sep-2025
- 75% cumulative by 15-Dec-2025
- 100% by 15-Mar-2026

For salaried where employer TDS covers most tax, advance tax is usually not needed. Required if you have material capital gains, foreign dividend income, or interest income exceeding what TDS covers.

- Capital gains — advance tax due in the installment AFTER the transaction (Sec 234C proviso), not backdated.
- **Interest u/s 234B/234C** applies on shortfall.
